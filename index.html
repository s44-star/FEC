Frontier Enterprise Council (FEC) is a social enterprise built on applied economic research. We investigate, test and measure practical ways to create productive employment and economic opportunity in underserved communities across Africa and the Caribbean.

The question we are working on

How can AI, modern technology, global outsourcing, enterprise development and other economic mechanisms create more productive employment and economic opportunity in the communities that need them most?

We do not assume the answers. We investigate, run real-world experiments, measure the results, and scale the approaches that work.

What FEC is, and what it is not

FEC is a social enterprise with applied economic research at its core. We start from a question about employment, not from a service to sell or a quota to fill.

  • A recruitment agency

    We do not start with candidates to place. We start with real demand for work and test whether it can become productive employment.

  • A growth or marketing agency

    We are not hired to raise one company's sales. We look at a community's employment problem and test what changes it.

  • A consultancy

    We do not write recommendations and leave. We run the experiments and measure what happens.

  • An accelerator

    We do not run cohorts of start-ups. Supporting enterprises is one of several mechanisms we test.

  • A conventional NGO

    We are a social enterprise. We aim for jobs that are economically productive, not employment that exists only on paper.

The communities we work for

Our priority is underserved communities in Africa and the Caribbean, especially:

  • Poor and economically underserved communities
  • Conflict-affected countries and communities
  • Refugee-hosting countries and displaced populations
  • People excluded from productive employment and economic opportunity

These communities are not research subjects. They are economic participants, workers, entrepreneurs and partners whose dignity matters.

How FEC works

We identify specific employment and economic problems, find real sources of demand, connect people and enterprises to opportunities, test interventions, and measure the outcomes.

  1. Question

    Start with a specific employment or economic problem in a specific community.

  2. Evidence

    Look for real sources of demand and for what already works.

  3. Experiment

    Run a small, real-world test of an intervention.

  4. Measurement

    Measure whether the work is real and productive.

  5. Learning

    Keep what the results support. Drop what they do not.

  6. Scale what works

    Expand only the approaches the evidence backs.

Employment mechanisms we are exploring

  • Global outsourcing and service exports
  • Remote employment
  • AI-enabled and AI-augmented work
  • Existing enterprise expansion
  • New enterprise creation
  • Technology-enabled self-employment
  • Supply-chain development
  • Skills linked to real market demand
  • Other evidence-based employment mechanisms

Outsourcing is one mechanism, not our entire mission.

We prioritise genuine, economically productive jobs, not artificial employment created to improve statistics.

From real demand to real work

A job only lasts if someone needs the work done. So we start by looking for genuine demand, including from employers abroad, and then test whether people and enterprises in underserved communities can meet it.

  1. Real demand

    Employers with genuine work, buyers and supply chains, including international outsourcing and service exports.

  2. FEC

    We identify the problem, test whether the demand can become productive work, and measure what happens.

  3. People and enterprises

    Workers, entrepreneurs and local partners in underserved communities who can do the work well.

Where we want to get to

Our long-term impact targets. These are targets, not achievements.

  • 300,000 jobs created Target, not yet achieved
  • 200,000 people reached through rehabilitation Target, not yet achieved
  • 200,000–300,000 people reached through medicine distribution Target, not yet achieved

We have not achieved any of these. We will count only jobs that are genuine and productive. Rehabilitation and medicine distribution are pursued through Mahakal Rehab and Mahakal Lab, described below.
