The economic challenge

Enterprise growth does not always become productive employment. FEC exists to investigate why, and to find what changes it.

These are the challenges we are working on. We do not claim to have solved them.

  • Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) face barriers that limit their productivity and growth.
  • Businesses need practical ways to improve operations and reach economic opportunities.
  • Enterprise development does not always translate into enough productive employment.
  • AI and modern technology open opportunities that businesses need help identifying and putting into practice.
  • New opportunities can emerge through outsourcing, stronger supply chains and productive business activity.

What FEC does

Four activities, one question: how does an enterprise become more productive, and when does that become sustainable, dignified employment?

Enterprise Development

Investigate SME challenges, identify the constraints on growth, and explore practical interventions that improve enterprise performance.

AI and Technology Adoption

Explore how AI, automation and modern technology can improve productivity, business operations and economic opportunity.

Employment Generation

Investigate how enterprise development, outsourcing, new business activity and stronger supply chains can support sustainable job creation.

Economic Research and Experiments

Study real business problems, develop interventions, test them in practice, and evaluate their economic and employment outcomes.

What FEC is, and what it is not

FEC is a research-led social enterprise. Its focus is private economic development: understanding how enterprises become more productive and generate sustainable, dignified employment.

  • A marketing agency

    We do not exist to raise one company's sales. We study what makes an enterprise more productive.

  • A recruitment company

    We do not place candidates to fill vacancies. We investigate how enterprises can sustain productive jobs.

  • An NGO

    We are a social enterprise. We look for employment that makes economic sense for the business, not only for the statistics.

  • A startup incubator or accelerator

    We do not run cohorts or programmes for start-ups. New enterprises are one area we explore among several.

  • A generic consultancy

    We do not hand over advice and leave. We test interventions in practice and evaluate what happens.

Where FEC focuses

African economic development is an important area of focus for FEC. These are the opportunities we are interested in exploring:

  • SME development
  • Women's enterprise and employment opportunities

  • Supply-chain development

African businesses, entrepreneurs and workers are partners in economic development. They are not case studies.

How FEC works: from assumption to evidence

We test before we scale. Five steps replace guesswork with evidence.

  1. Step 1Diagnose

    Understand the enterprise, its customers, operations, constraints and economic challenges.

  2. Step 2Identify

    Decide which problems are worth solving and which interventions could produce measurable benefits.

  3. Step 3Test

    Develop and run practical experiments instead of relying on assumptions.

  4. Step 4Measure

    Evaluate business outcomes, productivity, sustainability and employment effects.

  5. Step 5Improve

    Use the evidence to refine the intervention, and decide whether to continue or expand it.

A job that damages the business is not a good job. FEC looks for employment an enterprise can sustain.

Job creation has to be economically sustainable. FEC is not interested in adding unnecessary roles that hurt business performance.
